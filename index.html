On Chance, Time, and Distributed Authorship

In the perpetual now of computational systems, each timestamp becomes a seed— a precise coordinate in time that unfolds into linguistic possibility. The algorithm does not choose freely; it follows rules as strict as any score. Yet from this determinism emerges the unpredictable: constellations that shimmer briefly before dissolving into the next moment.

Drawing from Roy Ascott's vision of cybernetic art as collaborative meaning-making, this work positions the viewer not as passive observer but as temporal witness. You cannot control the flow. You cannot pause or rewind. You can only be present as language reorganizes itself according to laws both invisible and absolute.

Like Billy Klüver's engineered performances or John Cage's chance operations, the system embraces indeterminacy within structure. The vocabulary is curated, the rhythm measured, the palette deliberate— yet the composition at any given second remains unknown until time itself reveals it.

What you witness is neither random nor intentional. It is the space between: where human curation meets algorithmic selection, where memory (the lexicon) intersects with forgetting (each word's dissolution), where code becomes co-author of an ever-changing poem that exists only in the transient present.
